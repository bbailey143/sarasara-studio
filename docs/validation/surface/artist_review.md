# Surface artist review

## SURF-001 — the woven canvases, seen properly for the first time

- **Recorded:** 2026-08-20, in conversation
- **Surfaces:** `substrate.canvas.rough` and `substrate.canvas.linen`, both v0.1
- **Seen at:** Detail *Finest* — 570 × 420 across an 80 mm sheet, painting oil

**Artist's words:**

> Checking oil on canvas at Finest now — looks beautiful. […] the resolution is
> so helpful. And the rendered papers/canvases are looking really good.

### Why this only became judgeable now

The same canvas was rejected as unreadable earlier the same day. Nothing about
the cloth changed; the instrument did.

At the old 190-cell grid an 80 mm sheet gave roughly **2 cells per thread**,
which is at the limit of what any grid can draw — the weave collapsed into
moiré, and the artist could not tell whether the canvas was wrong or merely
zoomed. At 380 cells it is 4 cells per thread and reads as basketweave; at 570
it is 5.9 and is crisp.

The lesson is the one already written into the relay protocol: **check the
instrument before the engine.** Two rounds of judgement were spent on a surface
that was fine and a display that was not.

### What this does and does not settle

**Settled.** The woven surface reads as cloth to the artist at a resolution
where it can actually be seen, and paper and canvas are visibly different kinds
of surface rather than two settings of the same noise.

**Not settled, and not claimed:**

- No board mark was set. This is an impression of the surfaces while judging
  something else, not a scored behaviour.
- Both canvases remain **eye-guided**, not measured. Thread counts are standard
  trade figures — 12/cm for the rough duck, 20/cm for linen — not taken from the
  artist's photographs, which have no known scale.
- Colour is a guess. The supplied photographs are greyscale and cannot say what
  either cloth actually looks like.
- Valley depth and thirst cannot be seen in a photograph and remain stand-ins.
- The surface board rows do not exist yet. Paper and canvas have never been
  scored on their own — only judged through whatever was painted on them.

### Raised in the same breath, not built

> Still missing the major features of scratching and stuff.

Scraping back is `OL-10` on the oil board and is honestly marked not built.
Sgraffito through wet paint into the ground below is a real oil behaviour and
has never been attempted.
